Exercise: elemental measurements and dry-mass reporting

Goal

Connect the atomic absorption/emission signal path to an elemental quantity, recognise spectral overlap, and calculate copper per original dry mass with correct dilution and method-blank bases. Explain why elemental detection does not establish original molecular identity or digestion recovery.

Background

Atomic absorption measures attenuation of suitable source light by free atoms. Optical emission measures light produced by an excited population. Atomisation and excitation change the sample; a copper amount does not identify the original copper-binding molecule or oxidation state. Flame AAS, graphite-furnace AAS and ICP-OES differ in introduction and atomisation/excitation conditions.

The supplied elemental case calibrates introduced-solution copper in mg/L from emission counts. A dry sample is digested to a known volume, then diluted again before measurement. The method blank is a separate digest measured without that further dilution. Undo the sample dilution before subtracting the blank on a common digest concentration basis. All data and spectra are simulated.

By hand

B1. Write the signal path for AAS and for ICP optical emission. Which uses an element-specific light source, and which observes light emitted after plasma excitation? State one kind of chemical information atomisation does not retain. Hint

B2. A 0.2500 g dry sample is made to 50.00 mL digest. Its twofold dilution measures 0.40189 mg/L copper. The undiluted method blank is 0.001992 mg/L. Calculate corrected digest concentration, copper amount and mg/kg dry mass. Hint

B3. A second sample uses 0.2000 g dry material made to 25.00 mL. A fivefold dilution measures 0.0800 mg/L; undiluted method blank is 0.0100 mg/L. Calculate the result and explain why the blank is not multiplied by five. Hint

B4. A dissolved independent check recovers 100%. Does this establish complete plant digestion? Could a sample-specific neighbouring line still bias the sample while the simple check passes? Hint

Explore

The spectral model uses Gaussian lines on an offset axis, not authentic copper identification spectra. The isolated comparison line is idealised to share the same calibrated sensitivity without interference. Its output is a diagnostic apparent result, not validated reporting permission.

E1. Keep interferer height fixed and increase its distance from the target. Predict how the response at zero offset and apparent mg/kg result change. Explain why the total spectrum matters. Hint

E2. With visible overlap, switch blank subtraction off and on. Does blank correction remove the sample-specific overlap? Distinguish the two sources of excess response. Hint

E3. Select the ideal isolated line, vary the method blank and compare correct versus omitted blank subtraction. Export a scenario. Which evidence would be needed before treating a real alternative line as equally reliable? Hint

In R

Run the complete workflow at the project root in a fresh R session. A raw instrument offset represented by the calibration intercept is different from contamination measured by the whole-procedure method blank.

library(readr)
library(dplyr)
library(ggplot2)
standards <- read_csv("data/course/atomic-standards.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)
fit <- lm(emission_counts ~ copper_mg_l, data = standards)
a <- unname(coef(fit)[1]); b <- unname(coef(fit)[2])
qc <- read_csv("data/course/atomic-qc.csv", show_col_types = FALSE) |>
  mutate(estimated_mg_l = (emission_counts - a) / b,
         recovery_percent = 100 * estimated_mg_l / reference_mg_l,
         qc_pass = recovery_percent >= recovery_lower &
                   recovery_percent <= recovery_upper)
digests <- read_csv("data/course/atomic-digests.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)
blank_digest_mg_l <- (digests$emission_counts[digests$sample_id == "method_blank"] - a) / b
results <- digests |>
  mutate(candidate_measured_mg_l = (emission_counts - a) / b,
         in_range = candidate_measured_mg_l >= 0 & candidate_measured_mg_l <= 2,
         corrected_digest_mg_l = candidate_measured_mg_l * further_dilution - blank_digest_mg_l,
         copper_mg_kg = if_else(sample_id != "method_blank" & in_range & all(qc$qc_pass),
           corrected_digest_mg_l * (digest_ml / 1000) / (dry_mass_g / 1000), NA_real_))
ggplot(standards, aes(copper_mg_l, emission_counts)) + geom_point() +
  geom_abline(intercept = a, slope = b) +
  labs(x = "Copper in introduced solution (mg/L)", y = "Emission counts") +
  theme_minimal()
qc
results
write_csv(results, "output/atomic-methods-exercise.csv", na = "NA")

R1. Interpret the calibration basis and independent-check recoveries. Why are those checks not plant recovery validation? Hint

R2. Report A1 and A2 on the correct original dry-mass basis. Explain the method blank’s missing sample mass and why it has no mg/kg sample result. Hint

R3. List the main uncertainty and recovery evidence missing from these point estimates. Distinguish elemental copper from original copper species, and explain why a stable response is not proof of selectivity. Hint

Hints

B1: follow the photons

AAS transmits a source beam through free atoms. ICP-OES observes emitted light from an excited plasma population. Neither preserves molecular identity.

B2: make the bases agree

Multiply sample measured concentration by two, then subtract the undiluted blank. Use litres for digest volume and kilograms for original mass.

B3: the blank has its own history

The blank’s concentration already refers to undiluted digest. It did not undergo the sample’s later fivefold dilution.

B4: trace the check material

A dissolved reference bypasses the destruction and extraction of native plant material. It need not contain the sample’s interfering element or matrix.

E1: contribution at the selected wavelength

An interferer’s peak maximum is not the only place it contributes. Its tail can add signal at the target wavelength.

E2: two different backgrounds

The method blank models material introduced during preparation. The sample’s spectral neighbour is a separate contribution absent from that blank.

E3: an ideal alternative is not a free instrument correction

Compare to the known model truth, but remember a real alternative line needs its own interference/background evaluation and appropriate calibration.

R1: count the reference path

lm relates counts to copper in the introduced solution. The check does not recreate the original plant digestion.

R2: convert the denominator

One gram is \(10^{-3}\) kg. The blank is not a plant sample with zero or unknown true sample mass; it has no meaningful dry-mass denominator.

R3: distinguish a point estimate from validation

Consider sampling, native recovery, weighing, volumes, calibration, blank variation and spectral/matrix effects.

Solutions

Full worked solutions

B1. AAS: element-specific source → free-atom population → wavelength selection → transmitted-light detector. ICP-OES: sample aerosol → plasma atomisation/excitation → emission-wavelength separation → detector. Original molecular identity and oxidation-state distribution are not established by the resulting total elemental signal.

B2. Digest concentration is \(0.40189\times2-0.001992=0.801788\) mg/L. Copper amount is \(0.801788\times0.05000=0.0400894\) mg. Dividing by 0.0002500 kg gives 160.3576 mg/kg dry mass. The result differs slightly from the later full-precision R value because the hand inputs were rounded.

B3. Corrected digest is \(0.0800\times5-0.0100=0.3900\) mg/L. Amount is 0.009750 mg, and dividing by 0.0002000 kg gives 48.75 mg/kg dry mass. Multiplying the undiluted blank by five would invent a dilution it never had.

B4. No. A dissolved check can recover quantitatively while native plant copper remains incompletely recovered. A spectral neighbour present only in the sample can also bias a target signal while a simple check passes.

E1. At fixed peak height, moving the interferer away reduces its Gaussian tail at zero offset. Apparent introduced concentration and apparent dry-mass result approach the true contribution. Looking only at the selected count hides why it changed; the neighbouring spectrum gives relevant interference evidence.

E2. Blank subtraction removes the known digest blank on its own basis. It does not remove the sample-specific overlapping line. Both omitted blank correction and spectral overlap can bias high, but they require different chemical evidence and remedies.

E3. With ideal isolation and correct blank subtraction, the model always returns 160.00 mg/kg: native digest 0.800 mg/L × 0.05000 L / 0.0002500 kg. Omitting blank subtraction increases the result by blank mg/L × 200. The alternative line is assumed equal-sensitivity and interference-free here; real work requires appropriate calibration, background and spectral/matrix checks.

R1. The line maps emission response to mg Cu/L introduced solution over 0–2 mg/L. Checks recover about 100.37% and 100.77%, within the supplied 95–105% exercise rule. They do not establish native plant digestion, sample selectivity or a complete uncertainty budget.

R2. Full-precision results are 160.3587 and 300.4236 mg Cu/kg dry mass. The method blank has no original plant mass, so its mg/kg field is NA. It supplies a preparation background estimate rather than an elemental result for a hypothetical zero-mass plant.

R3. The point estimates assume quantitative digestion/transfer, appropriate calibration and selected-signal specificity. A complete evaluation would need sampling/recovery evidence and relevant uncertainties in mass, volume, calibration and blank response, including correlations where relevant. Stable counts show repeatability, not necessarily chemical specificity. Copper amount does not identify the original molecule or oxidation state.
