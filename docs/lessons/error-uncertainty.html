Reproducible is not necessarily close to the reference

Three methods repeatedly measure the same reference material at 10.00 mg/L. You will compare their bias and spread, then propagate uncertainty through a dilution. The goal is to explain what improves when readings are repeated and what remains shared by all readings.

Precision concerns closeness among repeated measurements under specified conditions. Bias is a systematic departure estimated against a suitable reference. Measurement uncertainty expresses doubt about a result under its measurement model. It is not the difference from a secretly known true value. An uncertainty budget needs the relevant sources, including sources not visible in a short set of repeat readings.

library(readr)
library(dplyr)
library(ggplot2)
measurements <- read_csv("data/course/precision.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)
summary_precision <- measurements |>
  group_by(method) |>
  summarise(n = n(), mean_mg_l = mean(measured_mg_l),
            sd_mg_l = sd(measured_mg_l),
            u_mean_mg_l = sd_mg_l / sqrt(n),
            bias_mg_l = mean_mg_l - first(reference_mg_l),
            .groups = "drop")
summary_precision

A and B both have SD about 0.0158 mg/L, but B’s mean is 10.50 mg/L: its estimated bias is +0.50 mg/L. C has mean 10.00 mg/L but SD about 0.316 mg/L. A mean close to the reference in one small series does not prove the method is unbiased in all matrices. Here reference uncertainty is neglected for the comparison; real bias evaluation must include it.

Which uncertainty does dividing by square root of n estimate?

For independent readings of one stable prepared solution, \(s/\sqrt n\) estimates the reading contribution to uncertainty in their mean. It does not describe the spread among future individual readings, nor does it reduce a shared standard-concentration error or lost analyte. Independence and the quantity being averaged matter more than the availability of sqrt().

If reading uncertainty is \(0.20/\sqrt n\) mg/L and another independent shared component is 0.10 mg/L, the combined standard uncertainty is \(\sqrt{0.20^2/n+0.10^2}\). More readings approach a floor of 0.10 mg/L, not zero. This is an illustrative model; the numbers are not estimated from the earlier reference-material table.

Propagate a transparent dilution model

A stock concentration is 100.0 mg/L with standard uncertainty 0.4 mg/L. A 5.000 mL aliquot has standard uncertainty 0.006 mL; final volume 50.00 mL has standard uncertainty 0.03 mL. Treat the three inputs as independent for this example. The model is \(c=c_sV_a/V_f\), giving 10.00 mg/L.

For small independent uncertainties, add squared contributions:

\[u(c)=c\sqrt{\left(\frac{u(c_s)}{c_s}\right)^2+ \left(\frac{u(V_a)}{V_a}\right)^2+ \left(\frac{u(V_f)}{V_f}\right)^2}\]

The sign of a derivative matters when covariances are present; in this independent example its squared contribution is positive. Two volumes from a shared calibration or common temperature effect need not be independent. The formula is conditional on the model, not a licence to ignore correlations.

c_stock <- 100
u_stock <- 0.4
v_aliquot <- 5
u_aliquot <- 0.006
v_final <- 50
u_final <- 0.03
c_final <- c_stock * v_aliquot / v_final
contributions <- c(stock = c_final * u_stock / c_stock,
                   aliquot = c_final * u_aliquot / v_aliquot,
                   final = c_final * u_final / v_final)
u_c <- sqrt(sum(contributions^2))
c(concentration_mg_l = c_final, standard_uncertainty_mg_l = u_c)

The contributions are 0.040, 0.012 and 0.006 mg/L. Combined \(u(c)=0.04219\) mg/L. If improvement is needed, reducing the dominant stock uncertainty is more promising than taking more readings of the already prepared solution. Eurachem uncertainty guidance

State what your interval means

A standard uncertainty is expressed on an SD-like scale. An expanded uncertainty is \(U=ku\) with a stated coverage factor and justification. A confidence interval for an estimated mean is another construction, using its sampling model. Do not call every “±” number a 95% interval.

For a normal mean based on independent readings, an appropriate t interval uses \(\bar x\pm t_{0.975,n-1}s/\sqrt n\). For the small dilution budget above, simply multiplying by 2 may be a useful approximate expanded uncertainty under further conditions, but those conditions and the intended coverage must be stated. A known significant bias is investigated or corrected with appropriate uncertainty; adding an arbitrary error bar does not correct it.

Keep unrounded quantities in code. A concise reported dilution result could be 10.000 mg/L with standard uncertainty 0.042 mg/L under the stated independent- input model. The reporting precision should match the uncertainty and purpose, not the maximum digits printed by R.

Numerical checks

The improved stock gives \(u(c)=\sqrt{0.020^2+0.012^2+0.006^2}\) = 0.02408 mg/L. Method C’s reading contribution is 0.1414 mg/L. Neither number incorporates sampling, matrix recovery or an unknown common bias.

Ask yourself: what quantity has SD 0.316 mg/L, what quantity has uncertainty 0.141 mg/L, and which effect would remain after infinitely many readings?
