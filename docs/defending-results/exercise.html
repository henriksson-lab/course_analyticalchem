Exercise: choose and defend an analytical result

Goal

Compile an auditable caffeine result from independent calibration, QC and preparation records. Report only supported values, distinguish candidate numbers from chemical claims, and explain uncertainty omissions and the next analytical action. This is integration of familiar chemistry and R, not a new modelling package.

Background

For the independent final case, area \(=4+40c\) over 0–30 mg/L caffeine in the final solution. A separate check contains 15.00 mg/L and has area 608. The supplied check window is 95–105% recovery. Original concentration is final concentration times final/aliquot volume. The dataset supplies separation and sample-block QC decisions, and standard uncertainties of final concentration.

Those uncertainties are separate inputs: the rounded ideal calibration points’ zero residual does not imply zero real uncertainty. Scaling them by dilution excludes volume, sampling and recovery uncertainty. Molecular identity also requires suitable selective evidence; a matching retention time or nominal ion mass alone is not unique proof.

By hand

B1. C1 has area 604 from5.00 mL made to50.00 mL. Separation and QC are accepted. Its supplied final standard uncertainty is 0.15 mg/L. Calculate final and original concentration and the dilution-scaled standard uncertainty. What does that uncertainty omit? Hint

B2. C2 of drink B has area 1604 from5.00 mL to50.00 mL. C3 is a newly prepared 2.00 mL aliquot of the same drink to50.00 mL, giving area 644. Calculate both candidates, choose what can be reported, and explain why C3 does not make C2’s old measurement valid. Hint

B3. C4’s area 484 lies within range and QC passes, but its candidate peak is unresolved. Can you report its algebraic caffeine concentration? What action would provide relevant new evidence? Hint

B4. A different instrument method gives a very high correlation with reference concentrations but increasingly positive differences. Does that establish acceptable agreement? Name the additional quantity or evidence you would examine. Hint

Explore

The embedded experiment keeps an old signal fixed while you plan a different aliquot or measurement condition. The new-measurement button creates a new record. Its model is intentionally idealised; the exploratory case uses the same final standard uncertainty 0.15 mg/L for each accepted new preparation, whereas the separate archived C3 dataset supplies0.16 mg/L.

E1. The initial drink B record is outside range. Select a2.00 mL aliquot without measuring. What changes? Now click Measure a new preparation and compare old and new records. Connect the result to B2.

E2. Plan drink A at 5.00 mL with failed independent QC, then measure. Its candidate may remain in range: which gate stops reporting? Next choose an unresolved separation and accepted QC for a new preparation. Explain why changing one evidence condition does not erase failures in earlier records.

E3. Export the history. Which columns are diagnostic candidates, and which are actual reportable outputs? Why should a withheld concentration export as missing rather than zero? Reset and explain which teaching state resets.

Solutions — E1–E3

E1. Selecting a new plan alone changes no recorded area, volume or decision. The old B record remains area 1604, final candidate40 mg/L and withheld. The new 2.00 mL measurement gives area 644, final 16 mg/L and original 400 mg/L. In this demo its limited standard uncertainty is 0.15×25=3.75 mg/L; archived C3 separately has 0.16×25=4.0 mg/L. They are two stated uncertainty-input scenarios, not a contradiction or a complete method budget.

E2. The new failed-QC record is withheld even if its final candidate is in range. A later unresolved record is withheld for selectivity. Controls represent supplied new evidence under a new scenario; they are not a licence to relabel an old failed measurement. Record histories retain those distinctions.

E3. candidate_final_mg_l and candidate_original_mg_l are algebraic values used diagnostically. reported_original_mg_l and its limited uncertainty are empty for withheld records. Empty means unsupported, not zero caffeine. Reset returns the teaching model to its initial plan and history; it does not model deleting a real laboratory audit trail.

In R

Run from the supplied project root with familiar CSV, transformation, plotting and regression functions. Keep the raw records unchanged.

library(readr)
library(dplyr)
library(ggplot2)
standards <- read_csv("data/course/case-standards.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)
qc <- read_csv("data/course/case-qc.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)
samples <- read_csv("data/course/case-samples.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)

R1. Fit the response model, compute independent QC recovery, then calculate sample candidates. Require range, supplied separation and all relevant QC evidence before filling reportable concentration and uncertainty fields. Preserve reasons for withholding. Hint

R2. Plot candidate-method minus reference for the paired results in method-comparison.csv. Explain which discrepancy needs investigation and why correlation alone would miss the problem. Hint

R3. Render a short report with the measurand, preparation table, calibration, QC, results, uncertainty scope and specific next action for each unresolved case. Optionally import your demo history and check that withheld outputs are missing. Distinguish ion identity evidence from quantity calibration. Hint

Hints

Hint — B1

Invert area 4+40c, then multiply both concentration and the supplied final standard uncertainty by the dilution factor. Do not call a standard uncertainty a 95% half-width.

Hint — B2

Apply the range to the final measured solution, before any original-sample back-calculation. A new aliquot requires its own measurement and record.

Hint — B3

The response model assumes the integrated area belongs to caffeine. Known coelution challenges that interpretation even with a perfect calibration line.

Hint — B4

Correlation measures association. Differences on the concentration scale expose bias relative to the comparison reference, with its uncertainty and scope.

Hint — R1

Use lm() with area as response. case_when() can attach a decision while if_else() protects concentration fields from being exported as unsupported results.

Hint — R2

The same sample_id has a reference and both method results. Subtract reference from each matched method result, then plot difference against reference concentration.

Hint — R3

NA describes an absent estimate, not a concentration of zero. A singly protonated ion near m/z195 can support a caffeine investigation but is not by itself a unique identity or quantitative calibration.

Solutions

Solutions — B1–B4

B1. \((604-4)/40=15\) mg/L final; factor 50/5=10 gives 150 mg/L original. The scaled standard uncertainty is 0.15×10=1.5 mg/L. It excludes volume, sampling, preparation recovery and other sources not in the supplied final estimate. It is not a 95% interval.

B2. C2 gives 40 mg/L final, outside the 0–30 mg/L working range. Its candidate original value 400 mg/L is not reportable. New C3 gives 16 mg/L final and factor 25, therefore400 mg/L original under accepted evidence. The equality of algebraic original values does not validate the old out-of-range signal.

B3. No: area 484 algebraically suggests12 mg/L final and 120 original, but known unresolved response makes that caffeine attribution unsupported. Improve separation or obtain justified additional selective evidence, then measure again.

B4. No. Plot method-minus-reference differences, evaluate reference uncertainty, sampling scope and the required performance. A systematic increasing discrepancy can coexist with extremely high correlation; the plot motivates investigation but does not prove the chemical cause.

Full worked R solutions — R1–R3

R1.

fit <- lm(area ~ caffeine_mg_l, data = standards)
a <- unname(coef(fit)[1])
b <- unname(coef(fit)[2])
qc <- qc |>
  mutate(estimated_mg_l = (area - a) / b,
         recovery_percent = 100 * estimated_mg_l / reference_mg_l,
         pass = recovery_percent >= acceptance_low &
                recovery_percent <= acceptance_high)
results <- samples |>
  mutate(candidate_final_mg_l = (area - a) / b,
         in_range = candidate_final_mg_l >= 0 & candidate_final_mg_l <= 30,
         reportable = in_range & resolved & qc_pass & all(qc$pass),
         dilution_factor = final_ml / aliquot_ml,
         original_mg_l = if_else(reportable,
                                candidate_final_mg_l * dilution_factor, NA_real_),
         u_original_mg_l = if_else(reportable,
           u_final_concentration_mg_l * dilution_factor, NA_real_),
         decision = case_when(!qc_pass | !all(qc$pass) ~ "withhold: QC",
                              !resolved ~ "withhold: coelution",
                              !in_range ~ "remeasure: outside range",
                              TRUE ~ "conditional result"))
qc
results |> select(preparation_id, sample_id, original_mg_l, u_original_mg_l, decision)
write_csv(results, "output/final-case-decisions.csv", na = "NA")

QC is 15.1 mg/L, or100.667% recovery, and passes. C1 and C3 report150 and 400 mg/L with limited standard uncertainties 1.5 and 4.0. C2 is outside range; C4 coelutes. Their reported concentrations and uncertainty fields are NA. Candidate fields remain diagnostic and must not be mistaken for reported measurements.

R2.

comparison <- read_csv("data/course/method-comparison.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)
differences <- bind_rows(
  comparison |> transmute(sample_id, reference_mg_l,
    difference_mg_l = hplc_mg_l - reference_mg_l, method = "HPLC"),
  comparison |> transmute(sample_id, reference_mg_l,
    difference_mg_l = nonspecific_uv_mg_l - reference_mg_l, method = "Unseparated UV"))
ggplot(differences, aes(reference_mg_l, difference_mg_l, colour = method)) +
  geom_hline(yintercept = 0, linetype = "dashed") + geom_point() +
  labs(x = "Reference result (mg/L)", y = "Method minus reference (mg/L)") +
  theme_minimal()

HPLC differs by 1–3 mg/L; unseparated UV differs by 13–45 mg/L with an increasing pattern. That suggests the latter method needs investigation for the intended use. The pattern does not itself identify a matrix mechanism or establish the reference as exact.

R3. A suitable conclusion reports conditional150 mg/L for A and 400 mg/L for B, with the stated limited uncertainties. C3 supplies B’s valid remeasurement; C2 remains outside range. C needs selective evidence and a new measurement. A peer should rebuild the report from saved inputs in a fresh R session.

Identity and amount require related but distinct evidence. For example, a singly protonated caffeine ion near m/z195 differs from its nominal neutral mass 194. Adducts, charge states and fragments require interpretation; the course spectrum is illustrative, not an authentic library match. The MS signal would also need a suitable calibration for quantification.

Where this leads

Carry the same traceability and withholding discipline into later laboratory reports. Choose a new measurement that resolves the actual uncertainty or selectivity problem rather than producing more digits from an unsupported model.
